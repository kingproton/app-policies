# 4 — Full reference list (100 songs)

**Reference window:** first Hot 100 top-10 weeks from the chart dated **2026-05-30** back to **2021-09-18**. That is the 100 most recent distinct rap songs, counting back from the chart dated 2026-11-21 (research cutoff 2026-11-22).

**Ordering rule:** by the chart week each song *first* reached positions 1–10, newest first. Ties within a week are sorted by position that week. Each song is counted once. Billboard's combined credits and remixes are followed, so Billboard's mid-run re-credit of "Carnival" from Kanye West to Ye counts as one song.

**Genre rule:** rap = Billboard placed the song on Hot Rap Songs or on a rap-only component chart (Rap Streaming Songs, Rap Airplay), or Billboard reporting says so. Billboard files each title under one core genre, so a song it charts on Hot R&B Songs, Hot Country Songs or Hot Rock & Alternative Songs is not on Hot Rap Songs. R&B-classified songs by rappers are therefore excluded and logged in 4d. (Hot Rap Songs is not a strict subset of Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs: a few pop-leaning rap titles appear on the year-end rap list but not on the year-end R&B/Hip-Hop list.)

**Rank and date sources.** Dates and ranks come from every weekly Hot 100 chart in the public `mhollingshead/billboard-hot-100` mirror of billboard.com. Two sources read directly on 2026-11-25 check them:
- **Billboard Canada's licensed Hot 100 pages** (`ca.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/<date>`, the site you named for the starting issue). It hosts 162 chart weeks from 2023-09-02 to 2026-11-21. In 161 of them every title matches the mirror at every position (two weeks differ by one title spelling), and the 25 rows per page that carry last week, peak and weeks on chart match too. The remaining page, labelled 2023-09-30, shows the 2023-09-23 chart; the following week's "last week" column agrees with the mirror. 7 later weeks are missing from that site and weeks before 2023-09-02 are not hosted.
- **Wikipedia's yearly "List of Billboard Hot 100 top-ten singles" pages**, which give each song's top-ten entry date.

For the 100 list songs: the first top-10 **date** matches Wikipedia's entry date for **100 of 100**, and the **date and rank** match the Billboard Canada page for **51 of the 51** whose week is hosted there. The other 49 ranks rest on the mirror alone. `www.billboard.com` was not read: its robots.txt bars Anthropic's agents, so the Billboard links below are citations, not pages opened in this study.

**Status: PROVISIONAL at the margins.** 86 of the 100 entries rest on a Billboard rap-chart figure read directly from a table (a year-end Hot Rap Songs list or a Hot Rap Songs peak in an artist's discography table). 14 entries rest on Billboard rap-chart rows known only through web-search summaries of Billboard pages (all Drake album cuts, whose Wikipedia tables have no rap column); marked †. 3 excluded candidates above the cut rest on indirect evidence. 7 more are excluded by deduction from year-end lists (strong, but not a weekly chart row). No dispute found in this pass changes the list; each remaining item could move the cut by one place. See *4e. Boundary and open items*.

## 4b. Per-track measurements

Measured on 2026-11-25 from three sources by signal analysis and by reading lyric records; nobody listened to the recordings, and nothing in this table comes from recollection. Method, checks and limits: `05-measurement-method.md`.

**Legend.**
- **Runtime:** duration of the Apple catalog track matched by title and artist. The lookup returns clean editions; where an earlier sourced runtime for the explicit version exists, the two agree within 2 seconds in every case.
- **Grid / felt BPM:** from Apple's 30-second preview clip, shown to the nearest half BPM. Grid = the fastest steady pulse that carries the beat (what a DAW project would be set to). Felt = the backbeat tempo (snare or clap on beats 2 and 4), which is the grid or half of it. **M** = clear in the clip; **L** = leaning; **I** = unclear in the clip, but only one reading lies in 55–150 BPM; **?** = unresolved, both readings shown. A clip is one section of the track: a beat switch elsewhere is not seen.
- **First line:** time of the first line in the LRCLIB time-synced lyric record that matches the track's duration. It can be an ad-lib, a spoken line or a transcribed sample, and a wordless intro before it is not counted.
- **First hook:** time of the first line of the main recurring section, with the number of lyric lines before it. *none* = no section recurs. **Hooks** = how many times it occurs. **Hook length** and **Verse 1 length** are in seconds, and in bars of four felt beats where the felt tempo is resolved (double the bar count on a doubled DAW grid).
- **Verse w/s:** words per second across verse sections (section boundaries from the repetition detector).
- **Timing basis:** **A** = the repetition detector and an independent AI read of the same lyric record agree; **J** = they disagreed and a second AI reader ruled; **G** = detector only; **X** = unresolved (not counted). Lyric timestamps are crowd-synced; treat them as good to about one second.

- **Basis:** **P** = the saved Wikipedia or Songfacts page text states it, and a second reader upheld that; **S** = a cited source the verifier did not reject (known through web-search summaries); **U** = cited, but not confirmed (never counted); **R** = unverified recollection (never counted); **?** = unknown. Unknown never counts as "no".
- **Opening** = what is heard first, as a source describes it. **Beat switch** = a source describes a change of beat or instrumental.
- **Return change** = coded from a cited description of what changes between sections: beat = a beat or bed change; voice = a new voice; register = the same rapper's register or character; delivery = a spoken insert or break; (one-way) = a switch that does not return.
- **Borrowing:** s = sample, i = interpolation, c = dialogue clip, r = self-reuse; (u) = uncredited. ✓ = confirmed by the verifier or by page text; no mark = cited by research, not flagged; U = could not be re-found (not counted).
- **2nd voice** = the Billboard credit names a second vocalist (producer-only credits such as Metro Boomin do not count).
- **Path:** **D** = debuted in the top 10; **C** = climbed in from the rank shown. wk = weeks in the top 10; tot = total Hot 100 weeks; AB = album-bomb cut (3+ songs by the same lead artist first entering the top 10 that week).
