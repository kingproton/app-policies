# 4 — Full reference list (100 songs)

**Reference window:** first Hot 100 top-10 weeks from the chart dated **2026-05-30** back to **2021-09-18**. That is the 100 most recent distinct rap songs, counting back from the chart dated 2026-11-21 (research cutoff 2026-11-22).

**Ordering rule:** by the chart week each song *first* reached positions 1–10, newest first. Ties within a week are sorted by position that week. Each song is counted once. Billboard's combined credits and remixes are followed, so Billboard's mid-run re-credit of "Carnival" from Kanye West to Ye counts as one song.

**Genre rule:** rap = Billboard placed the song on Hot Rap Songs or on a rap-only component chart (Rap Streaming Songs, Rap Airplay), or Billboard reporting says so. This study treats a listing on Hot R&B Songs, or on an R&B-only component chart, as an R&B classification and therefore not rap; that is a working rule, not a Billboard statement read in this study. R&B-classified songs by rappers are therefore excluded and logged in 4d. A listing on another genre chart (country, rock, dance, Christian) does not rule out rap: "All The Way" (#12) is on the 2025 year-end Hot Rap Songs list and on a country chart. (Hot Rap Songs is not a strict subset of Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs: a few pop-leaning rap titles appear on the year-end rap list but not on the year-end R&B/Hip-Hop list.)

**Rank and date sources.** Dates and ranks come from every weekly Hot 100 chart in the public `mhollingshead/billboard-hot-100` mirror of billboard.com. Two sources read directly on 2026-11-25 check them:
- **Billboard Canada's licensed Hot 100 pages** (`ca.billboard.com/charts/hot-100/<date>`, the site you named for the starting issue). It hosts 162 chart weeks from 2023-09-02 to 2026-11-21. In 159 of them every title matches the mirror at every position, and in 2 more one title is spelled differently (2024-01-20: #66 "Soak City (Do It)" against "Soak City"; 2025-11-29: #98 "Losing Streak" against "Losin' Streak"); in all 161 the 25 rows per page that carry last week, peak and weeks on chart match too. The remaining page, labelled 2023-09-30, shows the previous week's chart; the following week's "last week" column agrees with the mirror. 7 later weeks are missing from that site (2024-05-04, 2024-08-24, 2025-06-14, 2025-09-13, 2025-12-27, 2026-01-03, 2026-02-14); for those the chart comes from the mirror only, and no list song and no candidate in 4d first reached the top 10 in any of them. Weeks before 2023-09-02 are not hosted.
- **Wikipedia's yearly "List of Billboard Hot 100 top-ten singles" pages**, which give each song's top-ten entry date.

For the 100 list songs: the first top-10 **date** matches Wikipedia's entry date for **100 of 100**, and the **date and rank** match the Billboard Canada page for **51 of the 51** whose week is hosted there. The other 49 ranks rest on the mirror alone. `www.billboard.com` was not read: its robots.txt bars Anthropic's agents, so the Billboard links below are citations, not pages opened in this study.

**Status: PROVISIONAL at the margins.** 86 of the 100 entries rest on a Billboard rap-chart figure read directly from a table (a year-end Hot Rap Songs list or a Hot Rap Songs peak in an artist's discography table). 14 entries rest on Billboard rap-chart rows known only through web-search summaries of Billboard pages (all Drake album cuts, whose Wikipedia tables have no rap column); marked †. 6 excluded candidates above the cut rest on indirect evidence. 10 more are excluded by deduction from year-end lists (strong, but not a weekly chart row). No dispute found in this pass changes the list; each remaining item could move the cut by one place. See *4e. Boundary and open items*.

## 4b. Per-track measurements

Measured on 2026-11-25 from three sources by signal analysis and by reading lyric records; nobody listened to the recordings, and nothing in this table comes from recollection. Method, checks and limits: `05-measurement-method.md`.

**Legend.**
- **Runtime:** duration of the Apple catalog track matched by title and artist; where several editions matched, the one closest to the earlier cited runtime was preferred, so agreement with that runtime (within 2 seconds wherever one exists) is not an independent check. **(cited)** = no Apple catalog match (#13, #14, #30, #75): the runtime is the earlier cited figure and there is no clip, so the tempo cell shows a dash.
- **Grid / felt BPM:** from Apple's 30-second preview clip, shown to the nearest half BPM. Grid = the fastest steady pulse that carries the beat (what a DAW project would be set to). Felt = the backbeat tempo (snare or clap on beats 2 and 4), which is the grid or half of it. **M** = clear in the clip; **L** = leaning: the clip favours this reading by a small margin; **I** = resolved by range, not by the clip: only one of the two readings lies in 55–150 BPM, and it is shown even where the other scored slightly higher; **?** = unresolved: either both readings fit (both shown), or an unclear clip reading matches no published tempo at the same, half or double speed (shown as *clip … / listed …*). A clip is one section of the track: a beat switch elsewhere is not seen.
- **First line:** time of the first line in the LRCLIB time-synced lyric record that matches the track's length. It is the first *transcribed* line, not necessarily the lead artist: it can be an ad-lib, a spoken line or a transcribed sample (the kind is in table 4b-2), and a wordless intro before it is not counted.
- **First hook:** time of the first line of the main recurring section, with the number of lyric lines before it. *none* = no section recurs. **Hooks** = how many times it occurs. **Hook length** and **Verse 1 length** are in seconds, and in bars of four felt beats where the felt tempo is resolved (double the bar count on a doubled DAW grid).
- **Verse w/s:** words per second across verse sections (section boundaries from the repetition detector).
- **Timing basis:** **A** = the repetition detector and an independent AI read of the same lyric record agree; **J** = they disagreed and a second AI reader ruled; **G** = detector only; **X** = unresolved (not counted); G and X do not occur in this table. **no matching record** = no LRCLIB time-synced record within 3 seconds of the track length (#78, #87). Lyric timestamps are crowd-synced; treat them as good to about one second.
- **—** = not measured or not applicable: no clip, no lyric record, verses missing from the record (#39), or no recurring section.

## 4b-2. Per-track structure

The per-track values behind the structure counts in `03-findings.md`. Everything here comes from the same time-synced lyric record as table 4b; the section map is **one AI reader's call**, a hook sung twice in a row counts as one long hook, and the last section is measured to two typical line gaps after the last lyric line (never past the end of the record). Tracks with no lyric record are left out.

**Legend and which finding each column backs.**
- **First line is** (M-4): the reader's label for the first transcribed line, judged from text: ad-lib, sample or another voice, spoken, sung, rap, or unclear. It is the weakest label in the study.
- **Return anchor** (M-3): the time of the earliest line of any block that comes back later (repetition detector). *= hook* means it is the hook itself.
- **Hook before verse 1** (M-3, M-8): yes when no verse section starts before the first hook.
- **Intro** (M-4): time before the first hook or verse, in seconds and in felt bars where the felt tempo is resolved.
- **Section order** (M-5): I intro, V verse, p pre-hook, H hook, q post-hook, B bridge, O outro, x other, each with its start time.
- **Verse lengths** and **Hook lengths** (M-5, M-6): every verse and every hook in order, in felt bars where the felt tempo is resolved, otherwise in seconds. Verse 2 against verse 1 and last hook against first hook are read from these.
- **Hook on return** (M-6): the reader's label for the words when the hook comes back.
- **Verse syll/s · hook w/s** (M-2): verse syllables per second (estimated from spelling) and hook words per second, inside the detector's sections.
- **2nd run**: a second, separate run of the reading and ruling. **=** means it reached the same values for hook presence, first line (within 1 s), first hook (within 2.5 s), hook count (within one) and hook-before-verse; otherwise the item that differs is named; *n/a* means that run left the track unruled. It is a consistency check and never changes a value.
- In `data/track_measurements.csv` these are the columns `m_first_line_kind`, `m_first_anchor_s`, `m_hook_before_first_verse`, `m_intro_len_s`, `m_section_order`, `m_verse_lens_s`, `m_hook_lens_s`, `m_hook_return`, `m_verse_sylls_per_s`, `m_hook_words_per_s` and `m_second_run`. The column `m_hook_first` is a different measure (the hook has at most three lyric lines before it), and `hook_before_first_verse` without the `m_` prefix is the earlier documented field, not a measurement.

## 4c. Per-track documented facts

**Legend.**
- **Basis:** **P** = the saved Wikipedia or Songfacts page text states the fact or names the work, and a skeptic upheld that. A P can come from a supported verdict, from a partly supported one whose quoted words are in the saved page (the detail that is not on the page is not counted), or from a new page statement; `05-measurement-method.md` gives the split. **S** = a cited source the earlier verifier did not reject, known only through web-search summaries; **U** = cited, but not confirmed (never counted); **R** = unverified recollection (never counted); **?** = unknown. Unknown never counts as "no". The reader, the skeptic and the earlier verifier were AI agents; no person read the pages.
- **Opening** = what is heard first, as a source describes it. **Beat switch** = a source describes a change of beat or instrumental.
- **Return change** = coded from a cited description of what changes between sections: beat = a beat or bed change; voice = a new voice; register = the same rapper's register or character; delivery = a spoken insert or break; (one-way) = a switch that does not return; none specific = the cited descriptions say nothing specific changes; **?** = no cited description of a return was coded (unknown, not "no").
- **Borrowing:** s = sample, i = interpolation, c = dialogue clip, r = self-reuse; (u) = uncredited; (credit not stated) = the source does not say whether it is credited; (no URL) = named by research with no source link. ✓ = stated in the saved page text; (S) = credited per the earlier verifier's web-search summary and not found on a saved page; no mark = cited by research, not flagged; U = could not be re-found (not counted); **?** = no borrowing on file (unknown, not "none").
- **2nd voice** = the Billboard credit names a second vocalist (producer-only credits such as Metro Boomin do not count).
- **Path:** **D** = debuted in the top 10; **C** = climbed in from the rank shown. wk = weeks in the top 10; tot = total Hot 100 weeks; AB = album-bomb cut (3+ songs by the same lead artist first entering the top 10 that week).
