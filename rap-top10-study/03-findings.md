# 3 — Findings behind the process

These findings describe what the 100 most recent rap Hot 100 top-10 songs have in common. None of them claims that a technique *causes* chart success.

**How to read the counts:**
- Every count is **k / n assessed**.
- *n* leaves out tracks where the fact is **unknown**. Unknown never counts as "no".
- A fact counts as *measured* only when a cited source supports it ("sourced").
- Unverified recollection is shown separately and never used to rank a pattern.
- My production judgment appears only in the last section and is labeled **[J]**.

**What was assessed:**
- All 100 songs were researched album by album or group by group. An independent verifier re-checked each group.
- Runtime is known for 98, and tempo for 75.
- Sourced opening/hook data exists for a minority (see M-5 and the limits section).
- Nobody listened to the recordings.

## Ranked patterns (most useful for writing, arrangement and delivery first)

### M-1. Two felt-speed worlds: the half-time pocket around 70 felt, or full-time bounce around 100–107

- **Count:**
  - **53/75** tracks with a published tempo use a half-time feel (grid ≈ 2× felt), median felt **71.5 BPM** (IQR ≈ 65–77).
  - **22/75** are full-time: **11** sit at 95–115 (bounce / pop-rap), most of the rest are verse-led beats below 95, and 2 are faster club tempos.
- **Examples:**
  - *Half-time:* "Barbie World", "I'm Dat N***a" and "Privileged Rappers" sit at **144/72**, the exact grid of the worked session. Also "Super Gremlin" 146/73, "Pushin P" 155/77.5, "Like That" 162/81, "Wait For U" 166/83.
  - *Full-time bounce:* "Not Like Us" 101, "TV Off" 100, "Hey Now" 102, "Squabble Up" 104, "Lovin On Me" 105, "First Class" 107, "Big Energy" 106.
  - *Verse-led full-time:* "Wacced Out Murals" 82, "Reincarnated" 91, "7 Minute Drill" 85, "Champagne Poetry" 87.
- **Try it:**
  - Decide which world you're in *before* writing.
  - Clap 2 and 4 at the felt tempo and say your central phrase over it. If it only lands at one speed, that's your world.
  - In the half-time world, write lines that resolve on felt beats 2 and 4 (the snare), with room on the back half of beat 4. In the bounce world, more syllables fit per beat, so lean on internal rhyme.
- **Exception that suggests a different choice:**
  - "Just Wanna Rock" (Jersey club, about 150 full-time, 2:03) and "Super Freaky Girl" (sample at about 133) ride fast, fully felt pulses with very few words per bar.
  - If your bars are dense, those tempos work against you. If your hook is a chant, they're an option.
- **Flags:**
  - The full-time group is concentrated: Kendrick Lamar accounts for **8 of 22** full-time tracks (four West Coast bounce cuts and four verse-led *GNX* and *Mr. Morale* cuts).
  - Tempo values come from published BPM listings, cross-checked across sites. Half/double-time readings are resolved by convention, so the felt values are inference where no source describes the feel.

### M-2. Borrowed recognition: a sample or interpolation the listener half-knows

- **Count:**
  - A documented sample or interpolation in **56/100** songs.
  - Among the songs that **climbed** into the top 10: **9/12**.
- **Examples (the borrowed element is the hook or the first thing heard):**
  - "Paint The Town Red" — Dionne Warwick, "Walk On By"
  - "Big Energy" — Tom Tom Club, "Genius of Love"
  - "Players" — Grandmaster Flash & the Furious Five, "The Message"
  - "Lovin On Me" — Cadillac Dale
  - "Houdini" — Steve Miller Band, "Abracadabra"
  - "Luther" — Luther Vandross & Cheryl Lynn, "If This World Were Mine"
  - "Barbie World" — Aqua
  - "Way 2 Sexy" — Right Said Fred
  - "Janice STFU" — a slowed, rewritten Lykke Li "I Follow Rivers" melody
  - "Wait For U" — Tems, "Higher"
  - "Circo Loco" — Daft Punk
- **Try it:**
  - As an independent artist, budget for clearance or build an *original* recognition device that works the same way.
  - Options: a motif you or your producer replay; a vocal chop of your own title phrase; or a regional sonic signature that's yours.
  - Put it in the first 4 grid bars and bring it back with every hook.
- **Exception:**
  - "Fukumean" (2:05, no documented sample) and "Pushin P" run on a catchphrase and cadence instead.
  - A slogan that people repeat can do the sample's job.
- **Flags:** samples are documented across many artists, so this isn't one-artist-driven. Some "sample" entries are vocal quotes or skits, e.g. "Search & Rescue" (a spoken clip of Kim Kardashian).

### M-3. The 12 songs that *climbed* into the top 10 look different, and they're the closest mirror for an independent release

- **Count:** 12/100 climbed in; 88 debuted directly. Compared with the debuts:
  - **Guests:** credited feature in **1/12** climbers vs **42/88** debuts.
  - **Samples:** documented sample in **9/12** vs **47/88**.
  - **Staying power:** median weeks in the top 10 **5** vs **1**; median total chart weeks **31** vs **14**.
- **Examples:**
  - "Paint The Town Red": 20 weeks in the top 10, climbed from #15.
  - "Super Gremlin": 17 weeks, from #16.
  - "Fukumean": 15 weeks, from #12.
  - "Big Energy": 14 weeks, from #11.
  - "Favorite Song": 9 weeks, from #11.
  - "Agora Hills": 7 weeks, from #35.
- **Try it:** treat your demo like a climber. Make it:
  - **solo**, with your voice carrying every section;
  - built on **one recognition device** that returns (M-2);
  - in a pocket you can repeat live.
- **Exception:**
  - "Euphoria" climbed with **6:24** and no conventional chorus. It rode a public feud.
  - "Noid" climbed from #43 to #10 on album-release momentum.
  - Context, not structure, carried both.
- **Flags:**
  - **Small sample (12).**
  - Doja Cat has **3 of 12** climbers ("Agora Hills", "Paint The Town Red", "Vegas").
  - Climbing reflects sustained listening. Comparing climbers to debuts shows correlation inside this set, not a recipe.

### M-4. Most hits have a hook; the documented no-hook hits are statement records

- **Count:**
  - A conventional hook is **sourced in 26** and present-but-unverified in 24.
  - **No conventional hook** in **5** (all from sources describing the structure, none confirmed by listening).
  - Hook status is **unknown for 45**.
- **The five no-hook songs:**
  - "Euphoria", "7 Minute Drill" and "Hiss" are diss records.
  - "United In Grief" and "Champagne Poetry" are album openers.
  - "Hiss" uses a spoken intro and a spoken break between verses where a chorus would sit. "Euphoria" escalates through beat changes.
- **Try it:**
  - If you go hookless, build a **return anchor**: a spoken frame that comes back, a beat change, or one line repeated as a refrain.
  - Make the record's context obvious: the target, the occasion, the stakes.
- **Exception:** "Man At The Garden" turns one repeated claim into the whole structure, a *litany* hook. That's a middle path between chorus and no chorus.
- **Flags:** three of the five are diss records. Two come from the same 2024 Kendrick Lamar–Drake–J. Cole feud ("Euphoria", "7 Minute Drill"); "Hiss" is a separate diss record.

### M-5. What the listener hears first: more often a borrowed sound or a spoken frame than a rap verse; hook-first vs verse-first is split

- **Count:** a sourced opening exists for **24** tracks:
  - **12** open on a sample or interpolation;
  - **6** on a spoken or ad-lib intro;
  - **3** on a rap verse;
  - **2** on the hook;
  - **1** on a sung intro, and **1** on a producer tag ("Tomorrow 2").
- **Does the song open with its hook?**
  - Sourced: **6 yes / 6 no**.
  - Unverified recollection adds 6 yes / 7 no.
  - No first-vocal or first-hook **timestamp** was documented for any song.
- **Examples:**
  - *Sample-first:* "Super Gremlin", "Paint The Town Red", "Way 2 Sexy".
  - *Spoken or sung frame:* "Hiss" (a spoken intro), "Rich Flex" (Drake's sung call to 21 Savage).
  - *Hook-first:* "Family Matters" (a ~15 s opening chorus before the first beat switch), "Staying Alive".
  - *Verse-first:* "Pushin P", where Gunna and Future trade lines inside one opening verse.
- **Try it:**
  - Whatever comes first, make it **identifiable within the first 4 grid bars**.
  - Use a recognition device (M-2), a spoken line, or the title phrase. The worked session's two openings test exactly this choice.
- **Exception:** "Evil J0rdan" opens on an instrumental build. That only works when the beat itself is the recognizable element.
- **Flags:** coverage is thin, so treat this as directional. The audio handoff in file 05 measures it properly.

### M-6. The main change when a section returns is a *new voice*

- **Count:**
  - **43/100** have a credited featured artist.
  - **12** have a sourced voice-based return change.
- **Examples:**
  - "Pushin P": a hand-off verse, then Young Thug closes.
  - "Princess Diana": Nicki Minaj takes verse 2 *and* the chorus, so the returning chorus is in a new voice.
  - "FE!N": Playboi Carti drops to a deeper register.
  - "Way 2 Sexy": three rappers run one boast template.
  - "Like That": Kendrick's guest verse becomes the focal point.
  - "Reincarnated": verse 3 changes perspective, from past-life narration to a dialogue.
- **Try it (solo version):**
  - Make verse 2 sound like a different speaker: lower your register, change your persona or point of view, or switch pockets.
  - Or trade lines with your own double-tracked voice for four bars.
- **Exception:** climbers mostly do it alone (**1/12** with a feature). Their change on return comes from the arrangement and the recognition device, not a guest.
- **Flags:** featured-artist structures are concentrated in album-bomb cuts (26 of the 43).

### M-7. Beat switches are an album-cut device

- **Count:**
  - A **sourced** beat switch in **14** tracks, plus 3 by recollection.
  - Unknown for 70.
  - **10 of the 14 sourced** switches are on Drake-credited songs.
- **Documented positions:**
  - "TV Off": about **2:00**, cued by Kendrick shouting the producer's name as the part-2 percussion fades in.
  - "Family Matters": about **0:15**, after the opening chorus, with further switches for later verses.
  - Others ("Meltdown", "First Person Shooter", "Rich Flex", "Jimmy Cooks", "7 Minute Drill") have no documented time.
- **Try it:**
  - On a single, use the *small* version: a one-felt-bar drop or a vocal-only bar before the last hook.
  - Save a full beat switch for a statement record, or for verse 2 of a long song.
- **Exception:** the climbers and pop-rap singles ("Lovin On Me", "First Class", "Big Energy") have no documented switch. They loop one bed and change the vocal layer instead.
- **Flags:** **Dominated by Drake** (10/14 sourced switches are Drake-credited, including "Meltdown" with Travis Scott). The other four are Kendrick Lamar ("TV Off", "Euphoria"), J. Cole ("7 Minute Drill") and Metro Boomin/Future ("Superhero").

### M-8. Runtime: album-bomb cuts run long; everything else sits nearer 3:15–3:25

- **Count:**
  - Median **3:30** (IQR 2:58–4:08; n=98).
  - **25/98** are 3:00 or under; **31/98** are 4:00 or over.
- **By group:**
  - Album-bomb cuts: median **3:42**.
  - Other songs: **3:17**.
  - Climbers: **3:21** (n=11).
  - Drake-credited: **3:59**, vs **3:21** for everything else.
- **Examples:**
  - *Shortest:* "Barbie World" 1:49, "Just Wanna Rock" 2:03, "Fukumean" 2:05, "Pushin P" 2:16, "Lovin On Me" 2:18.
  - *Longest:* "Family Matters" 7:36, "Euphoria" 6:24, "Champagne Poetry" 5:36.
- **Try it:**
  - Build the demo between about 2:45 and 3:30.
  - Add a verse only if the new section brings a new voice or a new device (M-6, M-7).
- **Exception:** "Family Matters" and "Euphoria" are long because they're arguments; each new section is a new move in the fight.
- **Flags:** the long tail is Drake album cuts and feud records.

### M-9. What sources say about delivery (thin, but specific)

- **Count:** at least one sourced delivery observation for **55/100** tracks. Most are general. The specific ones:
  - melodic or sung delivery: 13 observations;
  - spoken or conversational: 7;
  - ad-libs: 4;
  - doubles or stacks: 3;
  - flow switch: 2;
  - silence or space: 2;
  - phrase placement: 2;
  - density: 1.
- **Examples:**
  - "Squabble Up": dense, technical bars delivered loosely.
  - "Hey Now": Mustard's bounce gives "the perfect pocket to run wild in".
  - "Agora Hills": a character voice and comic ad-libs.
  - "TV Off": the shouted producer's name *is* the transition cue.
  - "Shabang": guests appear only as ad-libs.
- **Try it:** pick **one** delivery signature per song (a register, a character, a cue shout) and use it at the same structural point every time.
- **Exception:** no source documents bar-by-bar phrase placement or the silence after punchlines. Those craft details are **unknown, not absent**.

### M-10. Concentration and sample-size flags (read before generalizing)

- **Chart pattern:**
  - **88/100** debuted in the top 10.
  - **58/100** spent exactly one week there.
  - **55/100** are album-bomb cuts, and 35 of those 55 lasted one week.
- **Artist concentration:**
  - **Drake** is lead artist on **38**.
  - Kendrick Lamar **12**, Future **9**.
- **Drill:** only **2** tracks are tagged drill, "Barbie World" and "Princess Diana", both Ice Spice. Every drill-specific recommendation in the process is therefore **judgment [J]**, not a finding.
- **The rap top-10 drought:** Billboard and the press reported no rap song in the top 10 from "What Did I Miss?" (July 2025) until spring 2026, and no rap song in the top 40 on the charts dated 2025-10-25 and 2025-11-01, a first since 1990. That gap is why the 100 reach back to September 2021.

## What could not be measured, and why

| Question asked | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First lead-vocal entrance (timestamp) | **Unknown for 100/100** | No audio access; lyric-timing hosts blocked; sources rarely state it |
| First hook timestamp | **Unknown for 100/100** | Same |
| Where phrases start/stop against the drums; where rhymes land | Documented descriptively for a handful only | Requires listening or annotated transcriptions |
| Silence after punchlines | 2 descriptive observations; no durations | Requires audio |
| Changes in drums / 808 / doubles / ad-libs on return | Sourced changes in 33 tracks, mostly voice or beat-switch level | Fine-grained layer changes need stems |
| Section lengths in bars | Not computed | Needs timestamps; would be bars = seconds × grid BPM ÷ 240 |

## Production judgment used in the process (not measured) [J]

- **Rhyme placement:** stressed rhyme syllables on or just after the snare; internal rhymes mid-bar; the last word kept clean with air after it. This is standard rap-writing craft, and the hits neither prove nor disprove it.
- **Silence before or after the punchline as an accent**, instead of an ad-lib.
- **The punch-in method:** hook first and comped, 4-felt-bar punch units, one-felt-bar pre-roll, never mid-chain, mic position marked.
- **Ad-lib density:** at most one per felt bar in verses, and only in gaps.
- **Rough-mix checks:** phone speaker, mono, car; high-pass the vocal at 80–100 Hz; carve 2–4 kHz in the beat under the lead.
- **Drill-specific choices:** pickups, 808-slide space, phrase-level punching.
- **Choosing 2:45–3:30 for a demo:** informed by M-8, but a judgment.

## Definitions and method notes

- **Grid BPM:** the DAW tempo convention; **felt BPM:** the rap-bar pulse. A half-time feel is coded when grid ≈ 2× felt. Published BPMs disagree by factors of two, so a felt value is a convention-based inference unless a source describes the feel.
- **Style tags** (trap, West Coast bounce, boom-bap/verse-led, pop-rap, R&B-rap, drill, jersey club, rage) were assigned by the research agents from source descriptions and credits. They're descriptive, not Billboard categories.
- **Hook-first** = the song's hook/chorus is heard before the first verse. Intros made of a sample, a tag or a spoken line count as "not hook-first" unless the hook itself comes first.
- **Verification:** each album or group was re-checked by an independent agent with its own searches. In the reference table, ✔ = confirmed, ✎ = corrected, ⚠ = conflicting sources. Claims a verifier could not support were flagged and are not used as "sourced".
