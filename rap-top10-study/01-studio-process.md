# 1 — Studio process: first beat loop → recorded demo

Built for King D Bird's way of working:
- you punch in, often finishing in one session;
- you write pointed imagery and dense internal rhymes;
- you favor pockets that leave room for the last word or an ad-lib to land.

**Evidence tags:**
- **[M-n]** = backed by a measured finding in `03-findings.md`, with counts out of the songs actually assessed.
- **[J]** = my production judgment. It was not measured, and the hits don't prove it.

Nothing here claims a technique *causes* chart success. The measured items describe what these 100 hits have in common.

## Step-by-step

### 0 — Session setup
- **Action:**
  - Set the DAW to the beat's grid tempo and write the felt tempo next to it.
  - Under a half-time trap feel, one felt bar = 2 grid bars. At 144 BPM, one grid bar = 1.667 s and one felt bar = 3.333 s.
  - Put markers at every planned block start, colour the hooks, and add a punch track.
- **Listen for:** the snare should land on felt beats 2 and 4. If it lands on 1 and 3, your felt grid is off by half a bar.
- **One fix:** nudge the loop start until the first kick sits on bar 1, beat 1, and set the tempo from the kick spacing, not the hats.

### 1 — Choose the beat and the pocket
- **Action:**
  - Before writing new bars, rap something you already know over the loop in three pockets: laid back (just behind the snare), on top, and double-time.
  - Keep the beat and pocket where your stressed rhyme syllables can sit on or just after the snare, *and* where the drums leave a hole at the end of every second felt bar for your last word or an ad-lib.
- **Listen for:**
  - kick and 808 hits colliding with your stressed syllables every bar;
  - hi-hat rolls eating your consonants;
  - whether the felt pulse is in a range you can stay articulate in for 16 felt bars.
- **One fix:** if the last word keeps getting buried, mute the 808 (or the kick) for the last beat of every second felt bar, or start lines a 16th later (on the "e" of 1).
- **What the hits show [M-1]:**
  - Felt pulses cluster in two pockets: a **half-time pocket around 65–84 felt BPM** (grid ~130–168) and a **full-time bounce pocket around 100–110 BPM**.
  - The full-time bounce pocket shows up in West Coast bounce and pop-rap.
  - Tempo alone doesn't separate the hits; it tells you which of the two felt-speed worlds you're writing in.
- **Verse-led vs trap vs drill:**
  - *Verse-led:* sparse midrange, felt ~85–100 with no half-time. Density reads because the hats aren't fighting you. [J]
  - *Trap:* grid 130–170 (felt 65–85). Cadence, repetition and space for ad-libs matter more than syllable count. [M-1][J]
  - *Drill:* grid ~140–146, syncopated kicks and sliding 808s. Lines start as pickups *before* the downbeat, and the 808 slide needs its own space. The drill sample here is tiny (see the M-10 flag), so this is judgment. [J]

### 2 — Find the central phrase
- **Action:**
  - Write 10 candidates of four words or fewer.
  - Choose the one that is an *image* (something you can see), fits in one breath, and has a stressed open vowel you can put on a downbeat or the snare.
  - Say it eight times over the loop before you write anything around it.
- **Listen for:** whether a stranger could repeat it after one phone-speaker listen, and whether it still means something on the eighth pass.
- **One fix:** cut a word; move the stress to the downbeat; swap a closed-vowel ending for an open one; or attach it to a sonic signature that comes back with it (a chant, a tag, a sample stab).
- **What the hits show:**
  - **[M-2]** A majority of the assessed songs lean on borrowed recognition: a sample or interpolation of a known record.
  - **[M-4]** Others lean on a repeated chant or litany, where one repeated claim *is* the structure.
  - **[J]** For you, clearance cost makes an *original* signature the practical version: a replayed motif, a vocal chop of your own phrase, a consistent tag.

### 3 — Decide: open with the hook or the verse
- **Action:**
  - Decide by the record's job.
  - **Single or short-form:** the title phrase should be heard within about the first 5 seconds, either as a spoken tag or by going hook-first.
  - **Statement record (verse-led):** open on the sharpest image and give the listener a *return anchor* by about 0:30. That can be a refrain line, a chant, the title as a verse-ending tag, or a beat change.
- **Listen for:** at 0:15, can a cold listener name the song or repeat one image?
- **One fix:** put a one-felt-bar, dry version of the title phrase into the intro (see Opening A in the worked session).
- **What the hits show:**
  - **[M-4]** Most assessed songs have a conventional hook.
  - **[M-4]** The documented exceptions are verse-led statement records, mostly diss or feud records, where the context did the hook's job.
  - **[M-5]** What listeners hear first is more often a borrowed sound or a spoken frame than a rap verse. Hook-first and verse-first are split about evenly where it's documented.
  - **[M-3]** The songs that **climbed** into the top 10, rather than debuting there, are the closest match to an independent release. They're mostly solo and built on a recognizable device.
- **Verse-led vs trap vs drill:**
  - *Verse-led:* image-first is viable, but plan the return anchor. [M-4][J]
  - *Trap:* hook-first or tag-first is the default. [J]
  - *Drill:* tag or ad-lib identity first, then a short hook. [J]

### 4 — Develop the verse
- **Action:**
  - Build in 4-felt-bar units: set-up, set-up, turn, payoff. The last word is the punch.
  - Run internal rhyme chains through the *middle* of bars and keep the final word clean.
  - Plan one pocket change per 8 felt bars: a density switch (e.g. a triplet run), a stop-time bar, or a register change.
- **Listen for:**
  - Do internal rhymes blur at full speed?
  - Does every fourth bar pay off?
  - Does the pocket change register as a change, or just as "more words"?
- **One fix:** remove one internal rhyme from each crowded bar and give the punchline a felt beat of air. Silence before the last word works as an accent.
- **What the hits show [M-6]:**
  - The most commonly documented "section-return change" is a **change of voice**, not a change of beat: guest verses, mic-trading, or one rapper changing register.
  - If you're solo, a register or pocket change is your version of a guest verse.
- **Verse-led vs trap vs drill:**
  - *Verse-led:* 16–24+ felt bars with flow switches every 8. [J]
  - *Trap:* 8–16 felt bars, with repetition and call-and-response with ad-libs. [J]
  - *Drill:* 12–16 bars, front-loaded with pickups, staccato endings. [J]

### 5 — Arrange the transitions
- **Action:**
  - Give every block boundary exactly one audible change: a drum drop, an 808 cut, a filter sweep, a beat switch, or a new voice.
  - Put the biggest change in the last third, before the final hook.
- **Listen for:** "same beat for three minutes" fatigue around 1:30, and whether each hook arrival actually lifts.
- **One fix:**
  - Mute the drums for the last felt bar before Hook 2.
  - Drop the full mix for one felt bar before Hook 3.
  - On a verse-led record, switch the beat for verse 2.
- **What the hits show [M-7]:**
  - Beat switches and two-part beds are documented on a meaningful share of the assessed songs. They cluster in superstar album cuts: Drake, Travis Scott, Kendrick Lamar.
  - Treat a full beat switch as an *album-cut* tool. A one-bar drop is the single-sized version. [J]

### 6 — Punch the lead (one-session workflow)
- **Action:**
  - Track the hook first (3 passes), then comp one lead and one double. Paste it into every hook slot.
  - For each verse, do two full passes for feel, then punch in 4-felt-bar units at marked breath points with a one-felt-bar pre-roll.
  - Punch on rests, never mid-rhyme-chain.
- **Listen for:**
  - energy, mic distance and room tone matching across punches;
  - breaths that are intact;
  - first consonants that aren't clipped.
- **One fix:**
  - Re-punch the whole 4-bar unit rather than one word.
  - Keep the same distance (mark the floor) and the same gain all session.
  - Crossfade at breaths, 10–20 ms. **[J]**

### 7 — Place ad-libs and doubles
- **Action:**
  - Ad-libs go only in the gap after a last word (usually the back half of felt beat 4), at most one per felt bar in verses.
  - Double the title phrase and hook end-words; save full stacks for the last hook.
  - Leave at least one spot where **silence** answers the punchline instead of an ad-lib.
- **Listen for:** any ad-lib stepping on a rhyme or punchline, and doubles smearing consonants.
- **One fix:** delete or move the ad-lib to the next gap. Pan ad-libs 30–60% L/R, 6–10 dB under the lead.
- **What the hits show [M-9]:** where sources describe ad-libs, they carry identity or comedy, and guests sometimes appear *only* as ad-libs. Placement inside the bar is undocumented: unknown, not measured. **[J]** for the placement rules.

### 8 — Check the rough mix
- **Action:** check on a phone speaker, in mono, and in the car. Bounce loudness-matched versions for A/B (around −12 to −10 LUFS integrated is fine for a demo; this is not mastering).
- **Listen for:** every word intelligible at low volume; the title phrase the clearest word in the song; the 808 not masking the 100–250 Hz body of your voice.
- **One fix:** high-pass the vocal at 80–100 Hz; dip 2–4 kHz in the beat under the lead (or sidechain it lightly); de-ess; ride each punchline up about 1 dB. **[J]**

## Where the decisions split: verse-led vs trap vs drill

| Decision | Verse-led | Trap | Drill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tempo / feel | Felt ≈ grid, ~85–110 (bounce lives at ~100–110) [M-1] | Grid 130–170, felt 65–85 [M-1] | Grid ~140–146, felt ~70–73 [J; tiny sample, M-10] |
| Pocket | 16ths on the grid; rhymes on 2 & 4 plus internal | Triplets and repeated cadences; room for ad-libs | Pickups into the bar; staccato; rests around 808 slides |
| Central phrase | Refrain line, or no hook with a return anchor [M-4] | Chant or title repetition; hook or tag first | Ad-lib/catchphrase identity plus a short hook |
| Opening | Sharpest image first | Hook or tag first | Tag or ad-lib, then pickup into the verse |
| Verse length | 16–24+ felt bars | 8–16 felt bars | 12–16 felt bars |
| Main change on return | Beat switch or new bed [M-7] | Voice change, 808/drum drop [M-6] | 808 pattern/slide change, drum drop [J] |
| Punching | Longer takes (8 felt bars) to keep momentum | Short punches are fine (cadences repeat) | Punch by phrase *including* the pickup |
| Ad-libs | Sparse; protect the bars | Frequent; call-and-response | Signature ad-libs in the gaps |
| Rough-mix risk | Midrange clutter hiding words | 808 masking the voice at low volume | 808 slides fighting the vocal low-mids |

## What this process cannot promise

- **Superstar album launches dominate the reference set.** 88 of the 100 debuted directly in the top 10, 59 spent exactly one week there, and Drake is lead artist on 38. Patterns that come mostly from album-bomb cuts describe what *superstar launches* sound like, not what makes a song grow. The climbers subgroup is the better mirror for you ([M-3]).
- **Listening-level details are unknown for most tracks.** Exact first-vocal times, the silence after punchlines, and 808 changes came from text sources only. `05-codex-handoff.md` explains how to measure them from the audio.
