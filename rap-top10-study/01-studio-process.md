# 1 — Studio process: first beat loop → recorded demo

Built for King D Bird's way of working:
- you punch in, often finishing in one session;
- you write pointed imagery and dense internal rhymes;
- you favor pockets that leave room for the last word or an ad-lib to land.

**Evidence tags:**
- **[M-n]** = backed by a measured finding in `03-findings.md`, with counts out of the songs actually assessed.
- **[J]** = my production judgment. It was not measured, and the hits don't prove it. Every rule of thumb, timing target and mix setting below is [J] unless a tag says otherwise.

Nothing here claims a technique *causes* chart success. The measured items describe what these 100 hits have in common.

## Step-by-step

### 0 — Session setup
- **Action:**
  - Set the DAW to the beat's grid tempo and write the felt tempo next to it.
  - Under a half-time trap feel, one felt bar = 2 grid bars. At 144 BPM, one grid bar = 1.667 s and one felt bar = 3.333 s.
  - Put markers at every planned block start, colour the hooks, and add a punch track.
- **Listen for:** the snare should land on felt beats 2 and 4. If it lands on 1 and 3, your felt grid is off by one felt beat (half a grid bar, 0.833 s at 144).
- **One fix:** set the tempo from the kick spacing, not the hats, then halve or double until the snare falls on felt beats 2 and 4.
- **Also try:** nudge the loop start until the first kick sits on bar 1, beat 1.

### 1 — Choose the beat and the pocket
- **Action:**
  - Before writing new bars, rap something you already know over the loop in three pockets: laid back (just behind the snare), on top, and double-time.
  - Keep the beat and pocket where your stressed rhyme syllables can sit on or just after the snare, *and* where the drums leave a hole at the end of every second felt bar for your last word or an ad-lib.
- **Listen for:**
  - kick and 808 hits colliding with your stressed syllables every bar;
  - hi-hat rolls eating your consonants;
  - whether the felt pulse is in a range you can stay articulate in for 16 felt bars.
- **One fix:** if the last word keeps getting buried, mute the 808 (or the kick) for the last beat of every second felt bar.
- **Also try:** start lines a 16th later (on the "e" of 1).
- **What the hits show [M-1]:**
  - Of the 70 songs with a resolved tempo, 51 read as **half-time** (felt median 71.5 BPM, middle half 66.5–77) and 19 as **full-time**: 10 in a bounce/pop-rap pocket at 100–111, 8 slower cuts at 80–92, and 1 at 133.
  - Every half/full call is inferred from listed BPMs by genre convention; no source describes the feel. Drake-credited songs supply 25 of the 51 half-time readings, and 8 of the 51 sit at grid 112–126, where a full-time reading is just as plausible.
  - Tempo alone doesn't separate the hits; it tells you which of the two felt-speed worlds you're writing in.
- **Verse-led vs trap vs drill:**
  - *Verse-led:* in the data, 5 of the 7 verse-led-tagged songs with a tempo are full-time at felt 85–92; the two exceptions are half-time ("Euphoria" 140/70, "Make Them Pay" 162/81) [M-1]. Sparse midrange lets density read, because the hats aren't fighting you. [J]
  - *Trap:* 26 of the 34 trap-tagged songs with a tempo sit at grid 130–170, felt 65–85 [M-1]. Cadence, repetition and space for ad-libs matter more than syllable count. [J]
  - *Drill:* the only two drill-tagged hits sit at grid 144 and 148 (felt 72 and 74). That's too few to call a pattern, so treat this as judgment: syncopated kicks and sliding 808s, lines that start as pickups *before* the downbeat, and space for the 808 slide. [J]

### 2 — Find the central phrase
- **Action:**
  - Write 10 candidates of four words or fewer.
  - Choose the one that is an *image* (something you can see), fits in one breath, and has a stressed open vowel you can put on a downbeat or the snare.
  - Say it eight times over the loop before you write anything around it.
- **Listen for:** whether a stranger could repeat it after one phone-speaker listen, and whether it still means something on the eighth pass.
- **One fix:** cut a word so it fits one breath, with the stress on the downbeat or the snare.
- **Also try:** swap a closed-vowel ending for an open one, or attach the phrase to a sonic signature that comes back with it (a chant, a tag, a sample stab).
- **What the hits show:**
  - **[M-2]** About half the songs (57 of 100) have a documented borrowing: a credited sample, interpolation or dialogue clip, or a reused beat. In several, sources say the borrowed vocal carries the hook ("Luther", "Super Gremlin", "Wait For U", "First Class"). Whether listeners already knew the borrowed record was not measured, and the other 43 songs are unknown, not "no".
  - **[M-4]** Some hooks are a repeated claim rather than a sung chorus: "Man At The Garden" repeats one line as a litany.
  - **[J]** For you, clearance cost makes an *original* signature the practical version: a replayed motif, a vocal chop of your own phrase, a consistent tag.

### 3 — Decide: open with the hook or the verse
- **Action:**
  - Decide by the record's job.
  - **Single or short-form:** the title phrase should be heard within about the first 5 seconds, either as a spoken tag or by going hook-first.
  - **Statement record (verse-led):** open on the sharpest image and give the listener a *return anchor* by about 0:30. That can be a refrain line, a chant, the title as a verse-ending tag, or a beat change.
- **Listen for:** at 0:15, can a cold listener name the song or repeat one image?
- **One fix:** put a one-felt-bar, dry version of the title phrase into the intro (see Opening A in the worked session).
- **What the hits show:**
  - **[M-4]** Where it was assessed, nearly every hit has a hook (52 of 58; only 20 of those are sourced). The six reported without a conventional hook, all unverified, are three diss records, two album openers and one two-part closer.
  - **[M-5]** Only 18 openings are sourced. The most common first sound among them is a sample, interpolation or recorded chant (10 of 18). Where the order is sourced, the hook arrives before the first verse in 8 songs and after it in 5, too few to call a rule.
  - **[M-3]** The 12 songs that climbed into the top 10 are closer to an independent release than the album-launch debuts. They're mostly solo (11 of 12) and mostly built on a documented borrowing (9 of 12), but it's a small, uncontrolled group.
- **Verse-led vs trap vs drill:**
  - *Verse-led:* image-first is viable, but plan the return anchor. Four of the six reported no-hook hits are tagged verse-led. [J]
  - *Trap:* hook-first or tag-first is the default. [J]
  - *Drill:* tag or ad-lib identity first, then a short hook. [J]

### 4 — Develop the verse
- **Action:**
  - Build in 4-felt-bar units: set-up, set-up, turn, payoff. The last word is the punch.
  - Run internal rhyme chains through the *middle* of bars and keep the final word clean.
  - Plan at least one pocket change per verse (every 8–16 felt bars): a density switch (e.g. a triplet run), a half-density bar, a stop-time bar, or a register change.
- **Listen for:**
  - Do internal rhymes blur at full speed?
  - Does every fourth bar pay off?
  - Does the pocket change register as a change, or just as "more words"?
- **One fix:** remove one internal rhyme from each crowded bar.
- **Also try:** give the punchline a felt beat of air. Silence before the last word works as an accent.
- **What the hits show [M-6]:**
  - When sources describe what changes as a section returns, the beat or bed changes more often (14 songs) than the voice (9 songs where a different person takes the section, 2 where the same rapper changes register or character).
  - [J] If you're solo, a register or pocket change is your version of a guest verse, and a bed change under verse 2 is the producer's version.
- **Verse-led vs trap vs drill:**
  - *Verse-led:* 16–24+ felt bars with flow switches every 8. [J]
  - *Trap:* 8–16 felt bars, with repetition and call-and-response with ad-libs. [J]
  - *Drill:* 12–16 bars, front-loaded with pickups, staccato endings. [J]

### 5 — Arrange the transitions
- **Action:**
  - Give every block boundary one audible change: a drum drop, an 808 cut, a filter sweep, a new bed, or a new voice.
  - Put the biggest change in the last third, before the final hook.
- **Listen for:** "same beat for three minutes" fatigue around 1:30, and whether each hook arrival actually lifts.
- **One fix:** mute the drums for the last felt bar before Hook 2.
- **Also try:** drop the full mix for one felt bar before Hook 3; on a verse-led record, change the bed for verse 2.
- **What the hits show [M-7]:**
  - A full beat switch is sourced on 10 songs: 6 Drake-credited, 3 Kendrick Lamar, 1 Metro Boomin/Future. Album-bomb cuts and other songs show them at the same rate (5 of 55 vs 5 of 45), and three are feud singles.
  - [J] Treat a full switch as a statement-record move. A one-bar drop is the single-sized version.

### 6 — Punch the lead (one-session workflow)
- **Action:**
  - Track the hook first (3 passes), then comp one lead and one double. Paste it into every hook slot.
  - For each verse, do two full passes for feel, then punch in 4-felt-bar units at marked breath points with a one-felt-bar pre-roll.
  - Punch on rests, never mid-rhyme-chain.
- **Listen for:**
  - energy, mic distance and room tone matching across punches;
  - breaths that are intact;
  - first consonants that aren't clipped.
- **One fix:** re-punch the whole 4-bar unit rather than one word.
- **Also try:** keep the same distance (mark the floor) and the same gain all session; crossfade at breaths, 10–20 ms. **[J]**

### 7 — Place ad-libs and doubles
- **Action:**
  - Ad-libs go only in the gap after a last word (usually the back half of felt beat 4), at most one per felt bar in verses.
  - Double the title phrase and hook end-words; save full stacks for the last hook.
  - Leave at least one spot where **silence** answers the punchline instead of an ad-lib.
- **Listen for:** any ad-lib stepping on a rhyme or punchline, and doubles smearing consonants.
- **One fix:** delete the ad-lib or move it to the next gap.
- **Also try:** pan ad-libs 30–60% L/R, 6–10 dB under the lead. **[J]**
- **What the hits show [M-9]:** sources describe ad-libs on only 4 songs. On one of them ("Shabang") the guests appear *only* as ad-libs. Placement inside the bar and the silence after punchlines are undocumented: unknown, not measured. The placement rules above are **[J]**.

### 8 — Check the rough mix
- **Action:** check on a phone speaker, in mono, and in the car. Bounce loudness-matched versions for A/B (around −12 to −10 LUFS integrated is fine for a demo; this is not mastering).
- **Listen for:** every word intelligible at low volume; the title phrase the clearest word in the song; the 808 not masking the 100–250 Hz body of your voice.
- **One fix:** dip 2–4 kHz in the beat under the lead (or sidechain it lightly).
- **Also try:** high-pass the vocal at 80–100 Hz; de-ess; ride each punchline up about 1 dB. **[J]**

## Where the decisions split: verse-led vs trap vs drill

| Decision | Verse-led | Trap | Drill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tempo / feel | Full-time, felt ~85–92 in the data (5 of 7 tagged songs; 2 half-time exceptions) [M-1]; aim ~85–100 [J] | Grid 130–170, felt 65–85 (26 of 34 tagged songs) [M-1] | Grid ~144–148, felt ~72–74; only 2 drill hits, so this is judgment [J] |
| Pocket | 16ths on the grid; rhymes on 2 & 4 plus internal [J] | Triplets and repeated cadences; room for ad-libs [J] | Pickups into the bar; staccato; rests around 808 slides [J] |
| Central phrase | Refrain line, or no hook with a return anchor [J] | Chant or title repetition; hook or tag first [J] | Ad-lib/catchphrase identity plus a short hook [J] |
| Opening | Sharpest image first [J] | Hook or tag first [J] | Tag or ad-lib, then pickup into the verse [J] |
| Verse length | 16–24+ felt bars [J] | 8–16 felt bars [J] | 12–16 felt bars [J] |
| Main change on return | Beat switch or new bed [J] | New voice [M-6]; 808/drum drop [J] | 808 pattern/slide change, drum drop [J] |
| Punching | Longer takes (8 felt bars) to keep momentum [J] | Short punches are fine (cadences repeat) [J] | Punch by phrase *including* the pickup [J] |
| Ad-libs | Sparse; protect the bars [J] | Frequent; call-and-response [J] | Signature ad-libs in the gaps [J] |
| Rough-mix risk | Midrange clutter hiding words [J] | 808 masking the voice at low volume [J] | 808 slides fighting the vocal low-mids [J] |

## What this process cannot promise

- **Superstar album launches dominate the reference set.** 88 of the 100 debuted directly in the top 10, 59 spent exactly one week there, and Drake is lead artist on 38. Patterns that come mostly from album-bomb cuts describe what *superstar launches* sound like. The 12 climbers are a closer but small, uncontrolled comparison ([M-3]).
- **Listening-level details are unknown.** First-vocal and first-hook times are unknown for all 100 tracks, no source gives the length of the silence after a punchline, and 808 and drum changes were not measured. `05-codex-handoff.md` explains how to measure them from the audio.
